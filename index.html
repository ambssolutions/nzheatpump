Sized for a Waikato winter

Inland Waikato gets hard frosts and thick morning fog. A heat pump that is too small runs flat out and still leaves the room cold, so we size for the coldest mornings, not the average ones.

What we look at before we quote

  • The size of the room and the height of the ceiling
  • Insulation, windows and which way the room faces
  • Where the outdoor unit can sit, for airflow and for the neighbours
  • How far the pipes need to run, and how they will be hidden
  • Whether your switchboard has room for a new circuit

Why sizing matters

The same room on the same frosty morning, with two different heat pumps. Switch between them.

Too smallThe unit runs flat out to keep up. Warm air stays near the heat pump, while the window side of the room stays cold. It is noisier and works harder than it should.

How an install goes

  1. 01

    Site visit

    We come to the house, measure up, and talk through where the indoor and outdoor units will go. You see the options before anything is decided.

  2. 02

    Written quote

    You get a quote that names the unit, the install and any electrical work. If the switchboard needs attention, it is on the quote, not a surprise on the day.

  3. 03

    Install day

    The outdoor unit goes on a solid base, pipes are run and covered, and the unit gets its own circuit. We clean up and test it with you in the room.

  4. 04

    Handover and paperwork

    We show you the remote, the filters and the settings that matter. You keep the electrical certificate for the work and the manufacturer warranty details.

Every job signed off

In New Zealand, electrical work on your home must be done by a licensed electrician. When we finish, you get the certificate the law requires for that work.

What you get at the end

  • A Certificate of Compliance for the electrical work
  • An Electrical Safety Certificate when new work is connected
  • Manufacturer warranty details for your heat pump
  • A tidy site, with offcuts and packaging taken away
